tibia

A typed Tibia-rules simulator in Spiral, compiled to C, Rust and WGSL (GPU) from one source, used to train agents that are measured against the best open-source Tibia bots on a public, reproducible benchmark: the OT Agent Arena.

Status 2026-10-21

27 .spi modules in src (plus src/standards.spi, the package's standards checked by eoie standards), 15 files in apps/arena (9 Lua probes and their shared client modules) and the server-map tool in apps/map.

  • Rules: geometry, vitality, coins, rng, cooldowns, damage, combat, creatures, experience, skills, blessings, wire.
  • Typed domain: quantity (phantom units), readiness (GADT witnesses), vocations (vocation-indexed

spells), catalog (existential spawns), views (HKT mapped and counted), terrain (layouts, incl. the scanned arena spot).

  • Decisions: policy, network (integer neural policy), search (evolution).
  • Simulators and entries: hunt with its hunt_sim tests, hunting_pack (the multi-creature simulator) with its pack_sim

and gpu_sim tests, and live.spi (the Lua entry for OTClient).

Tests name their targets with a reason: C as the oracle, WGSL for the GPU path, Lua for the live client, and Rust only where a test needs the training host.

Findings so far

Correction 2026-10-14: every live spiral-* run from 2026-10-13 11:48 until the fix ran with vBot's HealBot and TargetBot active alongside our policy (a bot setting the vBot probe left enabled; BENCHMARK.md, PR-1). The findings below come from the re-measurement on the fixed arena (PR-4, 30 runs per competitor, every run checked for foreign actions); the earlier live claims are withdrawn.

1. The simulator predicts live clear times and, mostly, potion use. Clear times matched in every live check. Potions took four corrections, each measured live or read from Canary's source: login protection keeps monsters off a fresh knight for 10 s; a simulator bug fielded three cyclops in small camps; monsters surround the knight instead of queueing (occupancy-aware routing, PR-7 holds out of sample); and monsters wander while the knight is protected, so every clear starts from a different geometry (before, every seed replayed one), with the cyclops swing pace measured at 2166 ms (mirroring_the_live_arena). Potions per unit of damage were always right; the misses were in hits taken. Against pooled live means the model now sits within the noise for rules and retreat at two and three cyclops (in-sample). Its out-of-sample check failed in one direction (PR-10): it predicted that preferring a monster already in reach costs 0.8 potions, and live it costs nothing (12.3 vs 12.5 for rules). Live, our policies drink alike. Since 2026-10-20 retreat steps cancel the pending swing as every walk does in Canary and the knight's think runs at the measured ~1083 ms (PC-6 confirmed both on fresh logs); with that the model gives ReachFirst 12.47 against rules 12.30, close to live, though that comparison is in-sample. Where nothing protects the fresh login (every arena since 2026-10-15), the model still predicts too many potions: rules 15.3 vs 14.4 (PR-11), retreat about 14.5 vs 12.8 (PR-12), the hunting ground 15.5 vs 12.7 (PR-15, whose kill pace it predicts well: 12.4 vs 12.8 kills). The retreat gap traces to crowding: the model's knight is pinned in the spot's dead end with three cyclopes, the live one keeps stepping (4.0 vs 11.5 retreat steps per clear). Update 2026-10-21: the arena bundle mirroring_the_current_arena (Canary's knight step speed, dance step and step formula on the map's ground speed, with the knight acting on the client's last view) closes the cyclops gap out of sample. PR-17, 40 fresh runs: rules 13.90 live against 14.55 predicted (t -0.87), retreat 13.25 against 12.81 (t 0.74); retreat steps 12.4 against 11.7 live. Since 2026-10-22 the bundle also carries two hunter-arena facts, each adopted by a rule written before measuring: three Canary timing facts (all3; PR-18 holds at 14 of 20 clears, where the bundle predicted 99 of 100), and arrows that stop only at projectile blockers, which lift predicted hunter hits from 14.9 to 21.0 per run (PR-19, 20 fresh runs: 20.45 live, t -0.40, and 15 of 20 clears; all3 alone rejects at t 3.86). The hunting ground's potion gap closed as noise on fresh hunts (PR-23, 2026-10-22: 14.8 live against 14.84 predicted at shield 11, t -0.05; PR-15's 12.7 was a low draw). Still open: the hunter clear time (55.9 s vs 60.5 s in PR-19), where live hunters walk faster at two to three tiles from the knight than the model's (about 1 tile per second against 0.44-0.66). A hunter run is decided by its first chase: live, the first target leads the knight 20 or more tiles away in about half the runs, and the run fails when the other hunters lose contact on such a chase; in the model such long chases are rarer (20%). Seven Canary movement facts were tested against this on 2026-10-21 and none passed in sample (TIBIA-FIDELITY). Since then, the model's long chases were traced to one path: once the knight lines up on a hunter's row, the hunter runs straight along it, the same way in every seed, while live hunters break off and still stay ahead (half of the live long chases end south, none of the model's do). Letting the model's hunters break off at random makes the knight catch every one, so the missing piece is what keeps a live hunter ahead after it turns. The likeliest answer is on the knight's side: when its target steps away from it, the live knight is still on its tile a second later in 98% of cases (the model's in 57-68%). Our competitor turns chase mode off while its target is adjacent, so Canary re-plans the knight's path only at the player's next one-second think and then waits a full step (TIBIA-FIDELITY note 85; a two-second follow timer, rule D30, was rejected). Modeled (rule D32), it reproduces the stalls (91% against 98% live) but the model knight then clears only 53 of 100 hunter runs against 72-95% live, so it stays off and the clear rate is the open question (notes 99-101). Retreat steps (15.1 vs 11.3 per clear) close when the knight's retreat steps follow the live competitor's own 650 ms pace: 11.7 predicted, 10.1 on 20 fresh runs (PR-20, t -0.54; potions 12.80 vs 13.32), adopted with the rest on 2026-10-22. The step count's spread is wide, so PR-20 could not reject the old 15.1 either, and its shape still differs: live runs are first pinned in the pocket at 1936,1349 after about 5 steps, the model's knight further north at 1937,1342 after about 8. The difference starts before any retreat: live, the cyclopses move off their summon tiles before the knight acts; the model starts them frozen there, so its opening is the same every clear. 2. The policy designed in the simulator does not win live. Stepping back when two cyclops reach the knight saved about 4 potions per clear in the first simulator; with monsters surrounding the knight as they do live, the saving shrinks to about 1 potion (5%), and live it uses 1.4 more than the plain rules (t 1.8, not significant). The earlier live win came from runs with vBot healing alongside. 3. Our rules policy is level with vBot. With enough runs (PR-9, 50 each, pre-registered) the Spiral rules policy drinks 12.5 potions per cyclops clear against vBot's 14.7 (difference -2.2, t -3.8), but the whole difference sits in the first 10 s: Canary ends a fresh login's protection when the player moves, turns, talks or uses an item, which vBot does and our probe does not, so vBot takes 9.1 hits in that window against our 1.6. After it the two take the same hits (60.1 vs 60.4). Re-tested with the protection ended for both (PR-11, 50 each): 14.4 vs 14.8 potions, no difference shown (t -0.6), identical hits (69.1 vs 69.2). Our three policies drink alike live (rules 12.5, ReachFirst 12.3, retreat 12.2). Against nExBot the retreat policy was narrowly better (12.2 vs 14.6 potions, PR-3a, t -2.14), but about half of that is the same login rule (nExBot talks right after login). The fair re-test (PR-12, 40 each, protection ended and both starting 2.5 s after login) shows no difference: 12.75 vs 13.93 potions, difference -1.18 (interval -2.42 .. 0.07, t -1.88). nExBot runs only with three arena shims (BENCHMARK.md PR-3a). 4. Learning wins little at this level of detail. In every scenario tried (open field, corridor, room, arena spot, mixed cyclops and trolls, potion budgets, respawning hunts) tuned threshold rules match or beat neural and scored learned policies. On a respawning hunting ground the rule that sweeps to the next spawn due beats every fixed route at a 60 s respawn, the evolved one included, but not at 30 s. With more monsters than the knight can fight at once, the tuned sweeping cavebot leads and the retreat rules trade experience for cost, and two knights coordinate best by simply following one shared rule together. That party finding rests on a duo model the first live duo hunts did not confirm (PR-24, 2026-10-22: 5.1 party potions against 7.56 predicted, 16.6 kills against 15.36): the model sent every cyclops at the first knight, while live the hits split about evenly. Canary's target order (the last knight to log in first, later arrivals in map-scan order) fixes the potions and the split in the model; it still kills about one troll per hunt too few, so the party sweep is rerun only after that.

The multi-creature simulator is bit-exact on WGSL too, checked since 2026-10-20 when Cells began honoring a WGSL test's status (before, every GPU test passed whatever it returned; all 28 GPU twins pass under the fix) (gpu_sim.spi: a full troll hunt, five arena-spot cyclops clears, and a five-minute hunting-ground hunt with the shortest-path router and its scratch arrays). With parallel lanes (one seed per GPU thread, 2026-10-14) every lane matches C exactly; a launch costs about 4 s of fixed setup, then about 0.23 ms per arena clear against 1.6 ms on one C core, so the GPU pays off from about 4000 clears per launch. Large lane counts now run in chunks of 1024 (the 16384-lane hang, WGSL-LANES-LARGE-DISPATCH-HANG, is fixed in the pack-1e compiler).

Simulator results

Protocol: everything is tuned on training seeds and judged on held-out seeds; the seed blocks are typed, so tuning on held-out seeds does not compile. Claims are Pareto dominance on (kills, profit) or a cost within a stated time cap. Profit books gold looted, 50 gp per potion and 14000 gp per death. The control is a random policy (each decision tick it drinks, engages, steps to a random tile or stands, with equal odds; once engaged the server keeps swinging, as live): on held-out seeds it earns 54310 experience on the hunting ground at -1.15 million gp, and clears the arena spot in 94 s with 72 potions per clear (one potion per second at most, as the server allows), far below every rule.

  • Targeting (three-troll pack, 20 seeds): finishing the current target first gets 330 kills; fighting whatever is in

reach gets 75 kills and 381 escapes. The live client switched to that order and killed all three trolls, where the old order killed one. A chasing policy gets 356 kills against 34 for a standing one (hunt_sim.spi).

  • Cyclops pack, open field (100 training and 100 held-out seeds): healing below 130 dies; 170 and above tie at 950

kills. Only the network warm-started from the cavebot tuned to heal below 160 dominates it, by 1550 gp at the same 955 kills. Kiting now walks the knight out of the cyclops' sight (monsters see 11 tiles), so they stop following and it kills only 300.

  • Terrain is the lever. In a one-tile corridor the same cavebot clears three cyclops with 6.5 potions and no

deaths, against 15.3 potions and 3 deaths in 20 clears in the open field. In a room with a corridor, rules evolved on 100 training seeds (ReachFirst, heal below 223 + 3 per attacker, step back at three attackers) dominate the fully tuned cavebot on 100 held-out seeds: 946 kills and -288217 gp against 942 and -477181.

  • Arena spot (the server map, radius 45, monsters routing around each other): rules evolved for the cheapest clear

within the tuned cavebot's time +15% choose ReachFirst targeting, heal below 88 + 26 per attacker and stepping back as soon as one monster reaches the knight. On 100 held-out clears they use 1070 potions against the tuned cavebot's 1702 (heal below 90), with one death against none, at 95.6 against 90.1 s per clear: cheaper, slower, not dominant. Before road speed applied at the spot (radius 30) the same search chose 96 + 8 per attacker for 1069 potions, no deaths, at 90.9 s.

  • Mixed camp (one cyclops, two trolls, arena spot): the cavebot tuned on this camp (heal below 80) uses 85 potions

in 100 held-out clears at 37.7 s. Rules evolved on this camp without knowing kinds use 74 potions at 37.6 s with no deaths, dominating it; kind-aware rules use 68 but die once. Rules evolved for three cyclops use 190 potions; counting only attackers that stand and fight (policy.temperate: trolls flee, cyclops do not) cuts them to 135.

  • Hunting ground (two cyclops and four trolls respawning every 60 s, the server map around the arena, five-minute

hunts, 100 held-out seeds): greedy one-tile steps stick behind walls there, so knight and monsters route with a shortest-path search (straight steps only: a diagonal costs three straight ones, and Canary's A* agrees), and every step costs what the server's ground under it says (roads, speed 100, are 1.6 times faster than the arena ground, speed 160; measured live in PC-1, confirmed by the pre-registered PC-2). With the cavebot's heal tuned on training seeds (120), standing at the start earns 104920 experience; a hand-drawn route 108270, a loop by the spawns 114470, a route evolved on training seeds with roads (a two-point shuttle between 121,86 and 117,102) 119280, and sweeping to the nearest live or next-due spawn 119950 (with the protection zones of the east buildings walled off since 2026-10-20). The rule that reads respawn timers leads at this 60 s respawn and ties at 120 s, but at 30 s the fixed routes beat it (hand route 133170, shuttle 133200, sweeper 126980): which walker is best depends on the respawn time.

  • Six cyclops on the hunting ground (more than the knight can fight at once; 100 held-out hunts, each cavebot's heal

tuned on training seeds): standing (heal below 100) earns 112700 with 2 deaths at -211081 gp; sweeping the spawns (heal below 130) earns the most, 126900, with 3 deaths at -296687 gp. Stepping back at two attackers while sweeping (the live retreat rules) earns 121050 at -166786 with no deaths: about 5% less experience for 44% lower cost, a choice of objective rather than a better policy.

  • Two knights on the six-cyclops ground (100 held-out hunts, party experience): the second knight acts through the

same code (the simulator swaps which knight is acting), and monsters chase and strike the nearer one. Both knights sweeping together earn 259350 at -180187 gp; splitting the ground into two territories earns less (248550) at 30% more cost (-233638), because each knight then faces the cyclops alone. Rules evolved for the duo (heal below 117 + 6 per attacker, never retreat) reach 261750 at -180890: within 1%, so one shared rule already captures the coordination.

  • Learned policies: a 12-gene scored policy (heal, retreat, targeting weights, step weights) whose start genome

reproduces the evolved rules found nothing better in 640 evaluations. With a 6-potion budget it clears 5.6% faster at the same deaths and potions, below the 5% cost bar. A respawning hunt with a potion budget does not separate policies either: above about 40 potions the budget never binds; below it the only decision that matters is when to leave, which a one-number rule captures.

Running

  • Set up: pwsh scripts/init.ps1 clones polyglot (which brings spiral) next to this repo, links both under deps/,

installs Mesa's software Vulkan on Ubuntu, and builds the WGSL runner into target/. -fast 1 only links and builds what is missing.

  • Test: pwsh scripts/build.ps1 runs every src/*.spi file with tests through deps/spiral/apps/cells/spi/run_notebook.ps1

and fails unless every test executed and passed (no failed, unsupported, skipped or missing rows); -Files geometry,views runs a subset. Notebook outputs go to notebooks/ (ignored by git, published by scripts/publish.ps1).

  • Standards: scripts/build.ps1 first runs SpiralCompiler format src apps/arena apps/map --check and stops on any layout

change; locally, eoie standards src apps/arena apps/map also checks the rules src/standards.spi declares (no comment lines, LF only, a target above every test, 120 columns, no floating point in 26 integer-only modules).

  • Live arena: the arena needs a local world outside the repo: Canary, MariaDB, OTClient, logs (default

~/tibia-local, or TIBIA_WORLD; MariaDB binaries from TIBIA_MARIADB_BIN, default scoop's). Ground rules

  • One novelty at a time, each with its own benchmark: fidelity before training, training before scale.
  • CPU and GPU runs of the same seed must agree bit for bit (integer simulation, counter-based RNG).
  • Every claim names its baseline, its control (random policy) and its budget; nothing is promoted on rhetoric.
